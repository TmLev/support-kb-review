The first two groups have different failure paths. The third is a separately tracked investigation, not a proven third root cause. The full ID lists above make the partition auditable. Each GitHub issue includes the same scoped count and evidence.

Shared parser: day and month reversed

The exported causeOf rule says that the shared parser reads month first while these tenants use day-first dates. For example, R-41200 says 7 April became 4 July, and R-41203 says 2 December became 12 February. App and email reports belong here even for tenants running the newer booking form. Route by tenant configuration and entry channel, not the intake browser locale or subject line.

Booking-v3: misleading field hint

The newer form prints MM/DD/YYYY but reads the tenant’s day-first format. People follow the hint and the form interprets their entry differently. This applies to form reports for Alder Physio, Copper Studio and Pale Harbour. R-41230 and R-41241 are examples. Changing only the shared parser would leave this form path wrong. Align the hint, validation and parsing with the tenant format.

One-day and temporal anomalies: evidence, not a diagnosis

desk-notes.json identifies R-41202, R-41209, R-41236, R-41344 and R-41620. Read their bodies before assigning a cause:

  • R-41202: Tuesday 12 May on the invite versus Monday 11 May on the calendar.
  • R-41209: June bookings are one day off; no dates or reproduction steps were supplied.
  • R-41236: an invite reportedly arrived before booking; this is a temporal anomaly, not proof of a one-day shift.
  • R-41344: Lisbon and Toronto recipients saw different days for the same meeting.
  • R-41620: Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday.

These observations do not establish a month/day reversal. Time-zone conversion, date-only storage and scheduling order are hypotheses to test, not confirmed causes. Collect original inputs, stored instants, named time zones, offsets, expected local dates and delivery timestamps. Reproduce each path before choosing a fix.
